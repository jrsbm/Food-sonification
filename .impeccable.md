# Design Context: Food Sonification Workshop

## Users
**Primary audience**: Food scientists and researchers presenting at conferences to introduce data sonification concepts.  
**Context**: Live presentation environment on a large classroom/auditorium screen to an audience unfamiliar with sonification.  
**Job to be done**: Showcase the range of sonification techniques (AM, FM, MIDI synthesis, percussion), then guide participants through a collaborative body percussion performance based on the data.

## Brand Personality
**Three words**: Scientific + Exploratory + Playful  
**Tone**: Engaging and approachable, not intimidating. Balance data rigor with artistic wonder. Invite participation and creativity, not just observation.

## Aesthetic Direction
**Visual direction**: Artistic & expressive with accessibility for presentations.  
**Theme**: Light mode (optimized for classroom projection on large screens).  
**Color palette**: Lighter, vibrant colors; avoid dull or dark tones that feel corporate or lifeless.  
**Visual references**: 
- PowerPoint's familiar, clear slide structure (audience expectation)
- FL Studio's music production UI (inspiration for audio/MIDI visualization sections)
- Scientific rigor meets creative energy

## Design Principles

1. **Presentation-first structure**: Each section feels like a clear presentation slide—title, controlled visuals, one clear point per section. Avoid overwhelming the audience with too many controls at once.

2. **Progressive disclosure**: Start simple (basic sine waves), gradually introduce complexity (ADSR, MIDI, spectral mapping). Let the audience grow with the concepts.

3. **Bright, energetic, artistic**: Use vibrant accent colors and generous spacing. This is about wonder and discovery, not clinical detachment. The interface itself should feel like a musical instrument.

4. **Interactive scaffolding**: Sliders, dropdowns, and buttons should feel intentional and responsive. Every control is a teaching moment.

5. **Data as beauty**: Spectrograms, waveforms, and MIDI data should be rendered beautifully and prominently—they're the stars, not background decoration.

6. **Rhythm and space**: Mirror presentation pacing with visual rhythm—tight sections for quick concepts, generous spacing for contemplative moments (like the spectrogram reveals).

## Technical Constraints
- **Framework**: Marimo (interactive Python notebooks)
- **Rendering**: Large classroom screens (wide, bright)
- **Interactivity**: Touch/click controls via sliders and dropdowns
- **Performance**: Real-time audio generation and visualization

## Differentiation
Make this **feel like a performance**, not a data analysis. The UI should invite interaction and make abstract spectroscopic data feel tangible and musical. Every visualization should tell a story: "This coffee spectrum has a rhythm. Listen to it. Now create your own."
